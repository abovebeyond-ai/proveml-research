# Vera review protocol

Version 1.2, 2026-09-12. A versioned statement of how a document is reviewed under Vera:
the steps, the rubric for resolving what the machine cannot check, and the criterion that
ends a round. A change to any of these is a new version below, never a silent edit.

## Scope

A document whose claims can be bound: numbers to the files that produce them, citations to
verbatim passages in archived copies, judgments to a registry. The first document reviewed
under this protocol is the ProveML paper (abovebeyond-ai/proveml-research, `paper/review`).

## The round

One round, in this order. The order is the protocol: resolving everything before editing
anything, and pushing once, is what keeps a round from churning.

1. **Pull** what people judged.
2. **Bind** every number to the file that produced it, regenerated by a recorded command,
   and every citation to a verbatim passage in an archived copy of the cited work. A
   number or passage the build cannot find refuses the build, and nothing goes past a
   refusal.
3. **Flag.** A model pass marks each statement that has no source, in every paragraph,
   the ones with bound numbers and citations included, only where the text changed since
   it last read them, with the question it would put to a reviewer. A proposal that reaches
   into a mark is cut to the part outside it; an answer with no list in it is a failed
   call, never a clean paragraph.
4. **Resolve every flag before touching any text.** One checker per flagged reading, with
   the repository and the archives as its world; a second, independent checker on any
   verdict that would change a sentence, and only a confirmed verdict may. Rubric:
   - Judge the passage **as displayed**, with the archive out of reach. The reader sees one
     quote; a source that supports the sentence elsewhere is not support.
   - A verdict of "stands" with a suggestion attached is not a stand. The suggestion is the
     finding.
   - For a work cited in several sentences with passages tagged per sentence: when none of
     a sentence's content words appear in the passages shown for it, the tagging is suspect.
   - Change text only on a verdict with evidence: a false statement, or a passage that does
     not carry the use. Leave "stands" alone.
   Every verdict is recorded against the paragraph it judged (`report/resolved.json`); a
   reading a checker settled is not asked again until its paragraph changes, as for a
   person's judgment.
5. **Edit once**, with the evidence attached to each edit, and no bound mark lost: the build
   counts marks before and after, and a paragraph that lost one refuses.
6. **Rebuild, re-flag the edited paragraphs, push once.** Log the round's measure.
7. **Repeat** until the pass records nothing and the measure holds for a round.
8. **Read**, once, by a person, on settled text; edit for meaning; one more pass over what
   was touched. A critic may draft, naming the concrete fault or staying silent.
9. **Judge.** Yes or no, by a named reviewer, on the page. A judgment is saved under a hash
   of exactly what it judged and carried across rebuilds by paragraph and field; a
   rewritten paragraph is a new question.

## The measure, and when a round ends

Per round: the number of flagged readings without an answer, plus the number of resolve
verdicts of false or partial. A round records this number; the loop ends when it is zero
and the next model pass records nothing. Step 5 runs once after that, and the factual loop
runs once more over what it touched.

The factual loop converges: the statements are finite, and each pass binds one, corrects it
or leaves it judged; a corrected statement re-enters closer to the sources it was checked
against. The style loop does not converge on its own: a model asked whether a sentence is
awkward always finds one, and every rewrite is new text whose readings are gone and whose
facts can slip. Hence step 5's single run.

## Measuring the loop

"The loop found two things" is an anecdote. What a product built on the loop needs is how
much it misses and how often it changes what was right, and the only way to know is to
plant errors and count. The measurement (proveml-research `paper/review/plant/`):

1. Plant known errors in a copy of the document, in four classes: a bound number changed;
   an unbound number in prose changed; an own statement about the mechanism, the runs or
   the artifacts made false; a citation moved to a sentence its work does not support. Each
   plant records what is true and where.
2. Run the copy through the same build, flag pass and resolver as the document, in a
   sandbox beside it (the live review, its judgments and its push untouched), with the
   document's own source out of bounds as evidence: a paper cannot be evidence for itself.
3. Count per stage: the build refuses (bound), the flag pass marks the planted span, the
   resolver's first checker catches it, the second confirms. Recall is what came back.
4. Precision is what happened on the unplanted readings of the same run: every confirmed
   change is classed by hand, and the classing is recorded (`adjudication.json`): the plant
   seen from a neighbouring reading; a defect of the document; a fault of the page; a
   change the author refutes. The last class is the false-change rate.
5. What the run finds about the loop itself is fixed before the next round and logged as
   "caught by planted run".

First run, 2026-09-12, on the ProveML paper: 43 plants (9 bound, 9 numbers, 14 statements,
11 citations); the build refused 9 of 9, the flag pass marked 33 of 34 prose plants, the
resolver confirmed 33 of 33 it was shown: 42 of 43. Of 56 confirmed changes on 242
unplanted readings, 13 were a plant seen from next door, 42 were defects of the paper (18
already known), one a fault of the page, none refutable. The same run found five faults of
the loop: paragraphs with a bound number or a citation had never met the flag pass; every
section's last reading was dropped from the resolver's list; a model answer with no list
in it was recorded as a clean paragraph; a proposal that reached into a mark was dropped
whole; and a cap of twenty spans cut the longest paragraph short. All five are fixed in
this version. Limits of the measurement: the plants are the author's, so their classes are
the author's, and an adversary's errors may fall elsewhere; the first checkers of the
planted run had the paper's other paragraphs in their world, so a plant contradicted by an
unplanted paragraph was easier than one that was not.

## Record

Every push, save and hand-back is an event on the review's record (`events.jsonl`); every
round's measure is appended beside it. The review root is signed and, when the document
goes out, anchored. "It converged" is that record, not a sentence in a paper.

## Limits of this version

- All readers of the first document were the author and agents the author ran. An
  independent reviewer has not yet judged a reading; the protocol does not require one, and
  it should.
- Step 4 is performed by a workflow an agent launches (proveml-research
  `.claude/workflows/resolve-readings.js`), not yet by the round script itself.
- The measurement has run once, on one document, with plants by the author. Its numbers are
  a first point, not a rate.
- The measure of a round counts flags and verdicts; it does not yet count what the planted
  run measures, so a round cannot tell by itself whether the loop got better or worse.
